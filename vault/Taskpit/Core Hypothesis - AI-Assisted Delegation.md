# Core Hypothesis - AI-Assisted Delegation

## Hypothesis

Student-organization leaders will find delegating through their own AI assistant connected to TaskPit more natural and convenient than their current workflow of asking AI to prepare task documents and then distributing those documents manually.

## Why this is the first test

Leaders already use AI to help write and organize work. TaskPit changes what happens to the output: instead of stopping at a document, the assistant can create structured, persistent records inside the platform.

The key question is not simply whether an AI can create a task. It is whether leaders prefer the connected workflow and find it useful enough to adopt.

## What the prototype should demonstrate

1. The leader explains work in natural language.
2. The assistant resolves missing critical details.
3. The assistant uses TaskPit through MCP.
4. Real task records are created and assigned.
5. The tasks appear in TaskPit's web UI.
6. The leader can inspect and edit them.
7. The leader can also create tasks directly in the UI.

See [[Leader Workflow - AI to Task Records]] and [[Prototype - First Working Slice]].

## Comparison baseline

The comparison is the leader's real current process: using AI to draft task documents and distributing them manually. The prototype should be judged against that workflow rather than against a hypothetical manual form.

## What success would mean

Technical success means the integration works end to end. Product success requires observing whether leaders understand the experience, prefer it, and want to use it for real work. These are separate questions.
