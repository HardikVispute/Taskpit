# The Problem - Team Execution

TaskPit is grounded in the experience of coordinating student-organization work, where assigning a task does not guarantee that the task will be completed well.

## Observed pain points

1. **Ignored assignments and missed deadlines.** Members may not act on work they have been assigned.
2. **Repeated chasing.** Leaders have to contact members again and again to learn the status of work.
3. **Weak contribution history.** It is difficult to maintain a dependable record of who actually did what.
4. **Fake submissions.** A member may submit something merely to mark a task done.
5. **Broken or incomplete submissions.** Submitted work may not meet the original requirements or may be unusable.
6. **Manual verification burden.** Leaders have to inspect work, request corrections, and decide whether it is acceptable.

## Important distinction

Creating a task is not the same as completing work. A submission is not the same as an accepted result. TaskPit should make those distinctions explicit in its eventual [[Task Lifecycle]].

## Product implications

- Task instructions need to communicate the expected deliverable clearly.
- Status should reflect the work's actual stage, not merely a claim of completion.
- Work history should preserve important events and decisions.
- [[Submission Quality and Review]] should help identify gaps while keeping human judgment available where needed.
- [[Core Hypothesis - AI-Assisted Delegation]] is the first prototype question, not proof that the broader execution problems are already solved.
